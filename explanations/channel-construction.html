Physical cooling-channel construction¶

build_channel_construction(circuit, reference_path) resolves a circuit’s nominal winding into the section it physically occupies. The same resolved geometry feeds the simulation, CAD, and the native 3D viewer, so a channel is sized once and drawn, meshed and solved from that one answer.

The three paths¶

A circuit carries three cylindrical (x, r, theta) paths, and they answer three different questions.

reference_path is the ideal contour carrying the nominal winding. Its radius is contour.r(x) exactly, and its angular rate comes from the placement’s helix law. Nothing about the cross section or the wall stack enters it, which is the point: two circuits asking for the same helix angle wind together and cannot drift apart, whatever sections they carry.

cross_section_base_point_path is where the section is anchored. For a squared section that is the midpoint of the coolant-facing floor; for a rounded one it is the inner lobe centre. Section planes, drawing arcs and CAD sketches are all built here.

cooling_channel_centroid_path is the coolant area centroid, computed from the resolved section rather than assumed to be half-way up the channel. The one-dimensional passage model takes its length, ds/dx and curvature from this path, so a channel that sits further out correctly runs further.

ThrustChamber builds them in that order: the winding first, then the placed sections, then the centroid the sections enclose. Swept volume pairs the section area with the reference path’s ds/dx, because that is the plane the area was measured in; using the centroid there would make the volume of a helically wound annulus depend on its helix angle, which it physically does not.

Saved geometry from before these definitions is rejected on load rather than renamed, because the stored coordinates meant something else. Regenerate it.

Main walls and liners¶

Wall accepts role="main" (the default) or role="liner":

tube_wall = psf.regen.Wall(material=copper, thickness=0.6e-3)
liner = psf.regen.Wall(material=silver, thickness=insert_thickness, role="liner")
# Pass walls=[liner, tube_wall] to CoolingCircuit.

List layers from hot gas to coolant, with all liners before the main walls. A circuit requires at least one main wall. Every main layer contributes to the channel-wall thickness; for rounded channels these are the layers forming the tubes and the material between neighboring coolant passages. A liner lies only on the combustion side and does not add to the tube-wall thickness. All layers still participate in through-wall thermal conduction.

circuit.channel_wall_thickness(x) and design input t_wall sum the main layers. circuit.hot_side_layer_thickness(x) and t_hot_layer sum the liners. circuit.total_thickness(x) and t_wall_total include both, describing the complete stack between hot gas and coolant. The wall-layer plot includes all layers on the hot side and assumes a closeout made from the main layers only.

Names and list positions do not assign roles. Existing calls and saved Wall objects without a role default to main walls; explicitly mark existing liners and regenerate saved simulations. The RL10 silver throat insert uses role="liner", while its stainless steel tube wall uses the main default.

Rib heat transfer¶

Set enable_fin and rib_material on each CoolingCircuit independently:

circuit = psf.regen.CoolingCircuit(
    # ... other circuit inputs ...
    enable_fin=True,
    rib_material=psf.common.solids.GRCop42,
)

enable_fin defaults to True. Set it to False to omit the fin heat-transfer enhancement while retaining the rib geometry. rib_material defaults to None, which uses the coolant-facing main-wall material. An explicit material supplies get_k(T) to the fin calculation only; wall conduction still uses the individual wall-layer materials. For rounded tubes an override represents an effective fin material, not an additional wall layer.

ThrustChamber no longer accepts enable_fin or overwrites circuit settings. Move any previous chamber-level setting onto each relevant circuit. Saved circuits retain their existing enabled/disabled setting and default to the wall material if they predate the material override.

Squared channels¶

Squared sections use planes perpendicular to the engine axis, for both straight and helical channels. Each origin sits on the base-point path, and the first in-plane axis points radially outward. Consequently the engine axis is at (-r_base, 0) in local plane coordinates. The channel floor and roof are exact circles about that point.

build_axis_normal_section_planes(path) provides these planes directly. They are also available through CoolingCircuit.section_planes(axis_normal=True) and ResolvedChannel.axis_normal_section_planes. The original flow-normal section_planes interfaces retain their behavior.

Let alpha = atan(dr_ref/dx) be the signed meridional slope angle, using the existing alpha convention, and let gamma be the helix angle measured from the meridian. The physical drawing dimensions are

height_transverse = h / cos(alpha)
pitch_transverse  = theta / cos(gamma_nominal)

Here theta is the simulation’s local angular pitch, already reduced by cos(gamma_nominal). Construction uses the stored nominal helix law. If a numerical layout changes a seed’s angular path, its derivative changes the local gamma used for rib projection; the nominal pitch allocation stays the same. A geometry-only circuit without a stored helix law uses the derivatives of its own path. Cylindrical derivatives avoid interpreting a coarsely sampled helix chord as an inward radial slope.

The complete wall stack also needs the meridional projection. The reference path is the bare contour, so the whole stack is projected onto it once:

r_base = r_ref + (t_hot_layer + t_wall) / cos(alpha)
r_roof = r_base + h / cos(alpha)

Rib projection¶

A simple wedge would produce a rib that thickens toward the roof. The construction instead intersects the floor and roof circles with the two faces of each rib.

Rib faces run along the local radial direction, so a transverse section shows ribs that point at the engine axis rather than leaning over. Writing u for radial and v for circumferential offsets from a rib’s own centre ray, its two faces are the parallel lines

v = +d
v = -d

A circle of radius R about the engine axis meets them at

delta = +/- pitch_transverse/2 -/+ asin(d / R)

so the floor and roof arcs run between the inward-facing intersections at the rib centres -pitch_transverse/2 and +pitch_transverse/2, joined by the straight faces. Invalid intersections and a closed channel opening raise errors.

d is not simply half the rib width. Both faces of a rib are helicoids: their separation inside a transverse plane is constant, but the material between them is that separation times the cosine of the helix angle seen locally, and one angular rate dtheta/dx is a steeper helix further out. A rib is therefore thinnest at the channel roof, and

d = sidewall_half_thickness * sqrt(1 + (r_roof * dtheta/dx)**2)

puts that thinnest full rib width at exactly 2 * sidewall_half_thickness, leaving the floor a little thicker. The rate belongs to the reference path, where the helix angle is measured: dtheta/dx = tan(gamma) / (r_ref cos(alpha)). With no helix this reduces to the usual constant-width rib between annular channels.

The rib face normal in (axial, radial, circumferential) coordinates is

n_rib = (r dtheta/dx, 0, -1) / sqrt(1 + (r dtheta/dx)**2)

at radius r. It has no radial component, because the face contains the radial direction.

These are local tangent-plane projections: alpha, gamma and dtheta/dx are held constant across a station’s section, and the wall is treated as locally straight. Contour curvature, taper and loft interpolation still introduce differences between the local model and the resulting solid. They fall on the safe side: on a tapering wall the neighbouring channel’s roof edge has already moved outward by the time two faces close on each other, so the solid keeps a little more material than the model asks for, never less. The station spacing controls the axial loft approximation. This construction avoids a CAD kernel in Python while retaining chamber-centered section arcs and explicit rib faces.

Choosing the rounded height input¶

Rounded sections accept the full internal coolant height by default. To use its older base-height parameter, set the option on the cross section:

cross_section = psf.regen.CrossSectionRounded(height_input="base")

Then CoolingCircuit(channel_height=...) supplies the base parameter. This is the value used directly as prof.h in commit ca6d36a, before the switch to full-height inputs. It excludes the rounded lobes and is distinct from the straight-side length, which is h_base*cos(theta/2).

Both lobes are tangent to the rays bounding the channel’s sector, so raising the outer lobe centre by h_base also grows its radius by h_base*sin(theta/2):

q_inner = d_lobe*sin(theta/2) - t_wall
H_full  = 2*q_inner + h_base*(1 + sin(theta/2))

d_lobe is the solved distance from the engine axis to the inner lobe centre (see below) and theta the sector angle in the section plane. The straight side length is a separate quantity, h_base*cos(theta/2).

Zero base height is valid: it produces the circular limiting section. Negative or nonfinite heights are rejected. Full-height input must still meet the geometric minimum 2*q_inner.

The section exposes full_height(profiles) and base_height(profiles) in either input mode. After chamber assembly, use circuit.channel_full_heights and circuit.channel_base_heights for the corresponding arrays on circuit.x_domain. The original channel_height(x), channel_heights, and design-input quantity h retain the supplied convention.

Height plots always default to full internal height, independently of the input convention:

psf.viz.PlotChannelHeight(thrust_chamber)                 # full height
psf.viz.PlotChannelHeight(thrust_chamber, height="base") # base height
psf.viz.PlotChannelHeight(thrust_chamber, height="both") # both, labeled

# The circuit selection option works with either representation.
plot = psf.viz.PlotChannelHeight(
    thrust_chamber, circuit_index=0, height="both"
)
report.add_tab("Channel geometry").add_figure(plot)

Squared channels have the same full and base height, so plotting both adds only one trace for those circuits. The wall-layer plot also uses full height. Native 3D and Onshape geometry use the section’s selected interpretation. The coolant centroid path uses the full height, after the local pitch and the lobe placement have been resolved. Equivalent base and full inputs therefore give the same section analytics, coolant path, and construction geometry when the other resolved design inputs agree. A helix or width law that depends on the input h sees the selected convention and may require a corresponding change to that law.

Rounded channels and Onshape¶

CrossSectionRounded builds its lobes in planes normal to the nominal winding, and both lobes are tangent to the two rays that separate a channel from its neighbours. The lobes are therefore sized at the radius they end up at: there is no outward translation afterwards, which is what used to leave the wrong amount of material between neighbouring tubes.

Where the lobes sit follows from the gas area. A locally prismatic channel meets an engine-axis-normal plane in the image of its section swept along the flow direction, so in a local radial/circumferential frame that projection is

radial displacement from normal-plane u:          u / cos(alpha)
circumferential displacement from normal-plane u: u tan(alpha) tan(gamma)
circumferential displacement from normal-plane v: v / cos(gamma)

Mapping the two sector rays back through that projection gives the sector the section really owns, with half-angle theta/2. The lobe-centre distance d_lobe is then solved so that the gas remaining inside the sector, outside the tube, equals that sector’s share of the ideal contour’s area, pitch * r_ref**2 / 2. Every channel keeps the gas area of the contour it replaces, and the tube exterior is tangent to the sector boundary, so neighbouring tubes touch. A liner extends the gas-facing arc inward from the tube and is included in the same solve.

Because the tube bulges inward between its tangent points and leaves a cusp of material at them, d_lobe is not the contour radius plus a wall thickness. On a strongly sloped wall it can even sit inside the contour, which is the correct answer for a tube bundle whose area-equivalent contour is what was specified.

At the minimum rounded height, the two arcs form a circle and FeatureScript omits the zero-length side lines.

The advanced example’s exporter writes schema version 6. Both channel types are exported in engine-axis-normal planes. The solver’s rounded sections remain flow-normal; regen.cad_geometry.transverse_construction provides a separate geometry-only representation for CAD and the native viewer. It intersects the locally prismatic section with a transverse plane, including both contour slope and helix angle, the shear when both are nonzero, and the rotated lobe frame. Circular arcs become elliptical arcs; straight sides stay straight. Zero helix only gives circular arcs where the contour slope is also zero. A curved CAD loft is still an approximation between those local sections.

Each channel carries sectionType: "squared" or "rounded". Each station contains a transverse plane, two affine arcs, and a circular-limit flag. An arc is represented by center + axisA*cos(t) + axisB*sin(t), with directed angle limits. Its JSON also includes principal-axis ellipse parameters for Onshape’s skEllipticalArc. Circles use skArc; zero-length sides are omitted at the rounded circular limit. FeatureScript lofts and patterns coolant seeds independently. Regenerate older JSON with the updated exporter and update the FeatureScript together.

The native viewer samples these same transverse arcs into polygons. Its n_arc_pts setting controls circumferential tessellation and stride controls the selected stations; the final station is always retained. Onshape retains analytic arcs and uses all exported coolant stations. Neither Python tests nor the native mesh renderer execute Onshape’s loft kernel.

Keep-outs¶

A surface channel’s angular pitch is by default an even share of the circumference among every occluding channel present at x. A ChannelKeepOut takes part of the circumference out of that share first:

theta(x) = (2*pi - blocked(x)) / n_occ(x)
blocked(x) = sum over keep-outs of count * width(x) / r(x)

Every channel in the span narrows by the same amount, not just the ones next to a sector. The one-dimensional passage model solves one representative channel per circuit, so that is the width it has to see. The wall a keep-out covers is booked to no channel. A circuit whose placement states its own channel_angular_width keeps it and ignores keep-outs.

In the numerical layout each sector is a pinned obstacle. The packer cannot open room on its own. A sector several pitches wide appears on top of a fully packed row, and relaxing that pushes channels through one another. So each circuit crossing a keep-out is first turned by a constant phase, less than one pitch, that puts the sector centres between channels. Then at every station the channels between two sector centres are scaled linearly onto the free arc between their edges. Where a sector has no width that map is the identity, so the channels leave the nominal pattern continuously along the taper. The relaxation that follows measures bending and slope from this seated shape, so it does not pull the channels back into the keep-out.

Sector counts join the layout’s symmetry. Ten sectors on a 280-channel circuit make it gcd(280, 10) = 10 wedges, so 28 channels are laid out instead of one, and they come out slightly different from each other. Keep-outs are axial. A helical circuit winding across one would have to change order, and is not supported.

Hot-wall export¶

The advanced and RL10 entry points share the same exporter and version 8 JSON contract. Every export includes the hot wall:

python examples/advanced/export_onshape.py
python validation/RL10/export_onshape.py --hot-wall-transition-fraction 0.02

Enable Construct hot wall in the shared Onshape FeatureScript. The result is a surface open at its axial ends, not a wall solid with thickness.

Plain walls and full-contour coverage¶

Where all active channels are squared, hotWall.revolves supplies the meridional chamber contour for a full revolution about the thrust-chamber axis. Straight and helical squared channels use the same surface. Each smooth run of the contour is exported as one spline through the contour’s own knots, which Onshape fits with skFitSpline, so the revolved wall is a single smooth face rather than a chain of short segments. Runs are cut wherever the meridian’s tangent jumps, keeping the independent slopes on either side of the throat, and a run left with a single interval falls back to the cubic Bézier that reproduces that interval exactly.

By default, these surfaces cover the union of the selected circuit spans. Overlapping or touching squared spans share a revolve; uncovered gaps remain open. If all exported circuits are squared, Cover entire hot wall appears in Onshape. It defaults off. Enabling it replaces the covered-span revolves with one revolution of the full chamber contour, including uncovered ends and internal gaps. The option is hidden when any exported channel is rounded. A circuit subset is evaluated on the channels included in its JSON.

Rounded and mixed spans¶

A span containing rounded channels uses hotWall.sections and hotWall.runs. Each run names the same channels in the same order at every section, and FeatureScript lofts each curve along its run separately. This retains the helical correspondence, including the squared pieces in mixed profiles. Squared-only spans elsewhere in the same chamber still use revolves.

Within each circumferential profile, squared pieces on the same contour join directly over the ribs. Squared/rounded and rounded/rounded neighbours are trimmed and joined by tangent cubic Bézier curves. The endpoint handles follow the adjoining tangents and have length one third of their separation. --hot-wall-trim-fraction controls how much of each arc’s parameter span is removed from each blended side, in (0, 0.5) (default 0.1). It is not a fraction of physical elliptical arc length. Closure and angular folds are checked before export.

Knit each hot-wall span joins the strips within each continuous loft span. If the kernel cannot knit them, the separate surfaces remain with a notice. The feature never attempts to connect separate spans across gaps.

Open interleave boundaries¶

When rounded channels are present, an internal boundary where circuits enter or leave a shared span is an interleave site. The wall is omitted on both sides of that boundary. The steady shared span between boundaries retains its rounded/mixed wall; the transition itself is left for manual CAD work.

--hot-wall-transition-fraction sets the total width of each empty zone as a fraction of the full contour axial length. This is the same convention the former blend zone used. The default 0.02 makes each gap 2% of the contour length, centered on its interleave boundary: 1% lies on each side. Python inserts exact profiles at the cut positions, independent of coolant sampling. Overlapping zones merge, and zones reaching a contour end are clipped. A span fully consumed by zones produces no hot wall. Squared-only exports need no interleave cuts because their plain contour is continuous.

Uncovered axial gaps are always left open in the default mode, regardless of channel type or gap width. Automatic axial transition bridges and the old absolute --hot-wall-transition-length-m control have been removed. The percentage transition control now sizes the empty interleave zone instead of a lofted bridge. Earlier hot-wall JSON must be regenerated for version 8.

The coverage diagnostic uses the union of selected circuit axial spans and warns if more than 10% of the full contour length has no circuit. It includes uncovered ends, counts overlaps once, and excludes the additional manual interleave gaps from that diagnostic.

Python callers can pass hot_wall_trim_fraction and hot_wall_transition_fraction to build_onshape_data or export_onshape_json in the example exporter.

The simulation’s flow areas, centroids, wall conduction, and effective hot-gas area multiplier remain unchanged. The native viewer displays transverse coolant geometry, not this additional hot-wall surface. Tangency is exact within each profile; free lofts do not guarantee 3D tangency or clearance between stations. Inspect the resulting CAD before relying on wall thickness. Hot-side ribs and an explicitly smooth silver-insert region remain outside this export’s scope.
