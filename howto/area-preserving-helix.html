Hold the coolant area constant with a helix¶

A squared channel of fixed height, rib width and count gets wider wherever the wall it wraps gets wider. On an engine with area ratio 8, an axial channel built this way has more than three times the throat area at the nozzle exit. To keep the area constant, wind the channel into a helix that steepens as the radius grows. The extra circumference then goes into the winding instead of the channel width.

This guide finds that helix by root-finding the helix angle against the area Pyskyfire computes. The result below has 40 channels, each holding the same coolant area to within one part in a million from the injector to the nozzle exit. Drag to rotate, scroll to zoom and right-drag to pan.

The complete script is examples/area_preserving_helix/area_preserving_helix.py.

Why the helix opens out with radius¶

Consider \(n\) squared channels of height \(h\) whose floor sits at radius \(r_b\). Each channel and its share of the rib fill an annular sector. On a helix of angle \(\gamma\), measured from the meridian, that sector’s angle measured normal to the channel is \(2\pi\cos\gamma/n\). Subtracting a rib of width \(2s\), where \(s\) is the sidewall_half_thickness, leaves approximately

\[ A \approx h\left[\frac{\pi\cos\gamma}{n}\left(2r_b + h\right) - 2s\right]. \]

With \(h\), \(s\) and \(n\) fixed, \(A\) is constant when \(\cos\gamma\,(2r_b + h)\) is. Taking a station where the floor radius is \(r_{b,0}\) and the helix angle is \(\gamma_0\),

\[ \cos\gamma = \cos\gamma_0\,\frac{2r_{b,0} + h}{2r_b + h}. \]

As the radius grows, \(\cos\gamma\) falls and the helix opens out. Since \(\cos\gamma \le 1\), no station can have a smaller radius than the reference. The reference is therefore the narrowest point, which is usually the throat.

This formula treats the wall as a cylinder. It misses two effects that grow with the helix angle. Both rib faces are helicoids, so the rib is wider below the roof than its nominal width (see Physical cooling-channel construction). On a sloping wall, the wall stack stands off along the normal, so \(r_b\) is larger than \(r + t_\text{wall}\). Used directly as a helix law on the engine above, the formula undershoots the target area by up to 10 % on the convergent, where the helix has reached 55°.

The root finder below matches the area Pyskyfire actually computes, so it includes both effects without modelling them.

Set up the geometry¶

Write a function that builds the chamber for any helix law, and a helix law that interpolates \(\cos\gamma\) from a table on the solver stations. A helix law is a design input that requests quantities by parameter name (see Design-Input Functions), so its argument must be called x.

xs, rs = psf.regen.contour.generate_contour(
    V_c=1.5e-3,         # Chamber volume [m³]
    AR_c=1.5,           # Chamber aspect ratio [-]
    r_t=0.025,          # Throat radius [m]
    area_ratio=8,       # Nozzle area ratio [-]
    nozzle="rao",
    R_1f=1, R_2f=2, R_3f=0.3,
)
contour = psf.regen.Contour(xs, rs, name="Contour")
wall = psf.regen.Wall(material=psf.common.solids.StainlessSteel304, thickness=0.8e-3)

def build_chamber(helix_angle):
    circuit = psf.regen.CoolingCircuit(
        name="Cooling Pass",
        contour=contour,
        coolant_transport=None,
        cross_section=psf.regen.CrossSectionSquared(sidewall_half_thickness=0.5e-3),
        span=[1.0, -1.0],
        placement=psf.regen.SurfacePlacement(n_channel_positions=40, helix_angle=helix_angle),
        walls=[wall],
        roughness=10e-6,
        channel_height=2e-3,
    )
    return psf.regen.ThrustChamber(
        contour=contour,
        combustion_transport=None,
        cooling_circuits=[circuit],
        n_nodes=400,
    )

x_stations = np.asarray(build_chamber(0.0).cooling_circuits[0].x_domain)

def tabulated_helix(cos_gamma):
    """Helix law interpolating cos(gamma) tabulated on the stations [rad]."""
    def helix_angle(x):
        return float(np.arccos(np.interp(x, x_stations, cos_gamma)))
    return helix_angle

def coolant_area(cos_gamma):
    """Coolant area at every station for a tabulated helix [m²]."""
    chamber = build_chamber(tabulated_helix(cos_gamma))
    return np.asarray(chamber.cooling_circuits[0].A_coolant_vals)

The transports are None because only the geometry is needed. In a real case, pass the transports you already have.

Root-find the helix angle¶

The area at each station depends only on the helix angle at that station. One build therefore evaluates the residual at every station, and a secant step applied to the whole array solves them all together:

cos_a = np.ones_like(x_stations)             # axial channel
A_a = coolant_area(cos_a)
A_target = A_a.min()                         # narrowest axial area, at the throat

cos_b = np.full_like(x_stations, np.cos(np.radians(30.0)))
A_b = coolant_area(cos_b)

while np.abs(A_b - A_target).max() > 1e-6 * A_target:
    # One secant step per station, all stations from the same build.
    # A station that has stopped moving takes no step.
    step = (A_b - A_target) * (cos_b - cos_a) / np.where(A_b != A_a, A_b - A_a, np.inf)
    cos_a, A_a = cos_b, A_b
    cos_b = np.clip(cos_b - step, 0.0, 1.0)
    A_b = coolant_area(cos_b)
    print(f"max area error: {np.abs(A_b - A_target).max() / A_target:.1e}")

Five builds in total, each well under a second.

  • The target is the largest area every station can reach, which is the axial channel’s area at its narrowest point. You can also set A_target directly. Stations whose axial area falls short of the target remain axial, because \(\cos\gamma\) is clipped at 1.

  • Iterate on \(\cos\gamma\), not \(\gamma\). The area is nearly linear in \(\cos\gamma\), so the secant step lands close to the root. Stepping in \(\gamma\) can overshoot to an angle where the rib fills the whole pitch, and the build then raises sidewall_half_thickness leaves no room for the channel.

Build the 3D view¶

Build the chamber once more with the converged law and save the scene. The chamber uses 400 stations and stride=1 draws every one of them, so the helix renders cleanly where it is steepest:

thrust_chamber = build_chamber(tabulated_helix(cos_b))
gamma = np.degrees(thrust_chamber.cooling_circuits[0].helix_angle_vals)
print(f"target area {A_target * 1e6:.3f} mm², helix angle {gamma.min():.1f}° to {gamma.max():.1f}°")

scene = psf.viz.Scene()
scene.add(psf.viz.ThrustChamberComponent(thrust_chamber, stride=1), name="chamber")
scene.save_html(output_dir / "engine-3d.html")

target area 6.420 mm², helix angle 0.0° to 68.0°

Limits¶

  • The tabulated law depends only on x, so several circuits can share the placement. It is tied to the contour, wall stack, channel height, channel count and n_nodes it was solved against. Solve it again whenever any of these change.

  • A change in channel count, such as under an overlapping return pass, changes the pitch abruptly. No helix law can hold the area across that step.

  • The independence of stations was checked for a single circuit on a SurfacePlacement. When several circuits are interleaved, keep the convergence printout: if a station’s area also depends on its neighbours, convergence slows down rather than failing silently.

  • Large helix angles lengthen the passage by roughly \(1/\cos\gamma\), which increases the pressure drop. At 68° the channel is about 2.7 times longer than an axial one. Check the pressure drop in the coupled analysis, not just the area.
