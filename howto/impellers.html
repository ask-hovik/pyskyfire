Create an impeller scene and report¶

Install the geometry and visualization extras:

pip install 'pyskyfire[impeller,viz]'

Impeller performs preliminary sizing and resolves physical blade inputs. build_geometry() calls pyTurbo Aero to construct the pressure and suction surfaces. ImpellerComponent places these surfaces in the regular scene API:

from pyskyfire.pump import Impeller
from pyskyfire.viz import ImpellerComponent, Report, Scene

impeller = Impeller(
    Q=0.01, H=100., n=10_000,
    shaft_diameter=0.020, inlet_hub_diameter=0.030,
    blade_count=6, blade_thickness=0.001, hydraulic_efficiency=0.90,
    phi1=0.20, incidence_deg=2., rho=1000.,
)
impeller.build_geometry()

scene = Scene(title="Impeller", show_axes=True)
scene.add(ImpellerComponent(impeller), name="impeller")
scene.save_html("impeller-scene.html")

report = Report("Impeller design")
report.add_tab("Geometry").add_iframe(scene.data_url)
report.save_html("impeller-report.html")

The complete runnable example is examples/impeller/impeller_report.py. It adds a design table, meridional plot, and blade-angle table to the report. It is designed at low specific speed with four full blades and one splitter per passage. Run it with --screenshot for a PNG preview. HTML generation does not need a display.

Duty, sizing and supplied efficiencies¶

Use SI units with rotational speed in rpm and blade angles in degrees. Q is delivered volume flow and H is the total head at the impeller outlet: the head the pump delivers plus whatever the volute or diffuser after it loses. Alternatively use Impeller.from_pressure_rise(mdot=..., rho=..., dp=..., n=..., **design_inputs), or size for a PumpDuty with Impeller.from_duty(duty, **design_inputs).

A PumpDuty states what the pump is asked to do, separately from how it is built. It can be written the way pumps are specified or the way an engine presents them, and stores the same thing either way:

from pyskyfire.pump import PumpDuty
from pyskyfire.thermo import Incompressible

PumpDuty.from_head(0.01, 100., 10_000, rho=1000.)          # Q, H, n
PumpDuty.from_pressure_rise(mdot=10., rho=1000., dp=9.8e5, n=10_000)
PumpDuty.from_states(fluid="Methane", mdot=22.8, p_in=5e5, T_in=135.3,
                     p_out=230e5, n=40_000)                  # real fluid
PumpDuty.from_stations(tank, pump_out, fluid="Methane", n=40_000)

fluid is a CoolProp name, a thermo.Fluid, or any thermo.EquationOfState, such as Incompressible(rho). H is the isentropic head: the enthalpy an ideal pump adds, over g. At constant density that is Δp/(rho*g). For a real liquid it is a little less, because the liquid compresses on the way through; for methane pumped from 5 to 230 bar the difference is about 2 %. The duty also reports the isentropic outlet flow Q_out and the specific speed.

Eye sizing retains phi1 = Cm1 / Ueye, solving annular continuity with a fixed wetted hub diameter. d1 can override the computed eye diameter. The shaft diameter and wetted hub diameter are separate inputs; no shaft stress sizing is performed. b2 defaults to Gülich’s mean outlet-width curve, Eq. (7.1).

Slip and the head closure¶

The flow does not leave along the blades. Gülich’s Table 3.2 predicts the swirl it does leave with: a slip factor from the blade outlet angle, the blade count, the blade blockage and the inlet diameter, a modified form of Wiesner’s correlation. outlet_slip() evaluates it on its own. The outlet triangles carry that swirl, and beta_flow_deg is the flow angle it implies, below the blade angle beta_metal_deg.

The design is closed on it. Exactly one quantity is solved so the predicted swirl is the swirl the head needs, c_u2 = g*H/(eta_h*u2) + u1*c_u1/u2 (Gülich Eq. T3.2.8):

  • By default the blade outlet angle is solved, which is the order of Gülich’s own procedure. The outlet diameter comes from one of psi, d2, u2 or a supplied meridional. With none of them it comes from Gülich’s head coefficient curve, Eq. (3.26), at fT (default 1.0; he finds 1.0 describes multistage pumps and 1.1 low-head single-stage volute pumps).

  • Give beta2_deg and the outlet diameter is solved instead.

  • Giving both over-specifies the wheel and is refused.

Eq. (3.26) describes whole pumps: their delivered head at a stage efficiency. H and hydraulic_efficiency here are the impeller’s, so the same wheel sits higher in the band by roughly the ratio of the two efficiencies. That is the reason to move fT or give psi outright.

At low specific speed the swirl a wheel can deliver has a broad, flat peak against the blade angle. A head above it cannot be had at any angle, and the error says what head coefficient can. The solved angle is always the one below the peak.

blade_count is an input. Gülich rejects empirical blade-count formulas as likely to violate the criteria that actually set it. Only full blades count toward slip; splitters are not in the correlation.

impeller.diagnostics reports what was solved, the resolved and correlated head coefficients, the mean-streamline slip prediction, the deviation angle (Gülich suggests at most 10 to 14 degrees) and the deceleration ratio w2/w1 at the shroud (de Haller: at least 0.7). None of them is enforced.

Efficiencies¶

hydraulic_efficiency is the impeller’s own, Gülich’s eta_h,La: the losses between its inlet and outlet. Each other loss is charged by the component it happens in, never lumped into one pump efficiency:

  • the volute or diffuser, by its own block, from the head it is handed;

  • disk friction on the wheel’s outer faces, by pump.DiskFriction (Gülich Table 3.6);

  • bearings and shaft seals, by the pump block’s mechanical_efficiency;

  • leakage, by volumetric_efficiency, which sets the internal flow to Q / volumetric_efficiency. Its default of 1 means no leakage.

blade_power is rho * g * Q_internal * H / eta_h, the work the blades do, or None without rho. network.PumpBlock combines the parts: its outlet is the impeller outlet, its shaft power is blade power plus disk friction over the mechanical efficiency, and the first two heat the fluid.

For a typical efficiency, Gülich’s Fig. 3.33 splits the losses of pumps near nq = 15 into about 2 to 3.5 percent of the head in the impeller against 13 to 18 percent in the collector, which puts eta_h,La near 0.97 where the whole stage is near 0.85.

The efficiencies are supplied assumptions, not predictions. There is no cavitation assessment or impeller loss prediction. The generated geometry is a preliminary design for inspection.

Design controls and conventions¶

  • blade_count, blade_thickness, hydraulic_efficiency, shaft_diameter and inlet_hub_diameter are required design inputs. beta2_deg is solved unless given.

  • beta1_deg=None derives hub, midspan and tip inlet metal angles from the unblocked annular velocity triangles, with incidence_deg added. An explicit beta1_deg overrides this calculation. inlet_prewhirl specifies absolute circumferential inlet velocity in m/s, positive with rotation.

  • Metal angles are measured from the backward circumferential direction toward the meridional direction: 90° is meridional. This implementation supports backward-swept blades. Incidence means metal angle minus relative flow angle.

  • Span runs from hub (span_fraction=0) to blade tip (span_fraction=1). The three defining profiles are interpolated by pyTurbo across that span.

  • wrap_deg is total circumferential sweep from leading to trailing edge, positive backward for rotation about +x. If omitted, it is estimated by integrating an interpolated metal-angle law along each meridional section. This integral sets the total wrap; pyTurbo constructs its own camber curve satisfying the endpoint controls.

  • Sections are stacked on the trailing edge: it lies in one axial plane at theta = 0, and the leading edge carries the spanwise wrap difference as sweep. Stacking rotates each section rigidly about the shaft, leaving metal angles, wrap and thickness unchanged.

  • BladeSettings.start slants the leading edge when given a (hub, tip) pair of stations. Slant it: a hub streamline is much longer in m' than a tip streamline, because dm' = dm/r and the hub runs close to the axis, so a leading edge on one station makes the hub section wrap far further than the tip section. That difference ends up as leading-edge sweep. Starting the blade further downstream on the hub, as a blade development is normally drawn, evens the wrap out. In the example, start=(0.2, 0.01) takes the spread from 65° to 6°.

  • The inlet velocity triangle and the inlet metal angles are evaluated on the leading edge, not at the inlet plane, with cm from unblocked continuity through the leading edge. The component of the through-flow crossing that line is what counts, so a slanted edge passes less than the area it sweeps. Eye sizing still uses the inlet plane and phi1.

  • tip_clearance is a distance in metres along the paired hub-to-shroud span. It trims the blade-tip boundary while preserving the displayed flow shroud.

blade_thickness is the total physical section-normal body thickness in metres. Supply a float or a function requesting any subset of the named scalar arguments chord_fraction, span_fraction, x, r:

def thickness(chord_fraction, span_fraction):
    return 0.0015 * (1 - 0.30*span_fraction) * (1 - 0.20*chord_fraction)

chord_fraction is normalized conformal meridional distance, with dm′ = dm/r, from main-blade leading edge (0) to trailing edge (1). x and r are in metres. Angle, incidence, prewhirl and wrap inputs also accept functions, with arguments span_fraction, x, r. Functions return a finite scalar and should be smooth. Angle and wrap functions are sampled at the three defining spans.

Thickness is fitted to pyTurbo’s surface controls. Leading and trailing edges close, so the thickness tapers there. Body thickness is checked at the defining profiles; fits exceeding 20% relative error away from the edge closures are rejected. geometry.thickness_max_relative_error reports this fit error. A sharp or rapidly varying function is unsuitable for the current fitter.

Optional BladeSettings(start=0.05, end=0.98, trailing_edge="rounded", angle_law="cosine") controls blade placement and wrap estimation. Start and end are fractions of the shared meridional station coordinate. start also accepts a (hub, tip) pair, which slants the leading edge; start_hub and start_tip always report both. A section can only begin on a span line, so a slant is a statement about which hub and shroud stations pair, and the passage is re-paired to carry that line: the hub keeps its stations and the shroud is resampled, then relaxed back to the original pairing downstream. A supplied meridional passage is re-paired the same way. Pairings that would push the shroud below the hub are rejected. Trailing edge can be "rounded" or "cut"; independent edge-radius inputs are not exposed because the supported upstream version does not reliably honor them.

Optional SplitterSettings(start=0.5, pitch_fraction=0.5, thickness_scale=1.0) adds one splitter per main-blade passage. It follows the downstream main-blade camber, starting at the specified main-blade chord fraction. pitch_fraction places it circumferentially between main blades. Its thickness function uses the main-blade chord coordinate, multiplied by thickness_scale.

Meridional passage and generated representation¶

The default passage uses smooth quarter-ellipse hub and shroud curves with an axial inlet and radial outlet. axial_length sets the shroud’s axial extent; otherwise a preliminary dimension-based default is used. Supply meridional=MeridionalContours(hub=hub_xr, shroud=shroud_xr) for custom paired (N, 2) arrays of (x, r) stations. Both arrays must have the same length, positive passage width and monotone axial/radial coordinates. Their endpoints must match the sized inlet radii and, if b2 is given, the outlet width; the outlet radius is taken from it and the blade angle solved. Pair stations across the passage; the shared station coordinate is normalized hub arc length.

MeridionalContours is the radial-impeller form of the shared pyskyfire.common.MeridionalPassage, so a passage also reports its area() and hydraulic_diameter() at each station and can record which wall stretches rotate (WallMotion) and where blades stop short of a wall (Clearance). The impeller does not fill those in yet.

impeller.dimensions, impeller.velocity_triangles and impeller.blade_definition are available before importing pyTurbo. Geometry generation is explicit and can be repeated at another resolution:

geometry = impeller.build_geometry(span_points=33, chord_points=129)
geometry.export_npz("impeller-geometry.npz")

The geometry contains the hub and shroud curves plus one main blade and, optionally, one splitter. Each blade has pressure and suction arrays shaped (span_points, chord_points, 3) in cylindrical (x, r, theta) coordinates; theta is in radians. Cartesian conversion uses (x, r*cos(theta), r*sin(theta)). The visualizer caps the blade ends and instances the blade mesh around the axis. Hub and shroud are flow boundaries, not a solid wheel or casing.

The adapter targets pyturbo-aero==1.3.8 and translates its angle, wrap, thickness and clearance conventions. This dependency is optional and imported only by build_geometry(). Native blade surface generation and the two old experimental impeller modules have been removed. Existing users should import Impeller from pyskyfire.pump, supply the required design inputs, and call build_geometry() before adding an impeller to a scene. Create a new Impeller when changing design inputs.
