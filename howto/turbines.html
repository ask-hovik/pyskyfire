Design an axial turbine¶

Blade generation needs pyTurbo, which comes with the impeller extra, and the scene needs the viz extra:

pip install 'pyskyfire[turbine,viz]'

A turbine is designed in three steps, each with its own object:

  1. TurbineDuty: what the turbine is asked to do.

  2. AxialTurbine: a meanline design that meets the duty and draws the hardware as a TurbineDefinition.

  3. TurbineDefinition.build_geometry(): the blade surfaces, and what can only be measured on them.

from pyskyfire.turbine import AxialTurbine, RowSettings, Stage, TurbineDuty
from pyskyfire.viz import Scene, TurbineComponent

duty = TurbineDuty.from_states(fluid="Methane", mdot=22.8, p0_in=160e5, T0_in=633.,
                               p_out=115.5e5, n=40_000)
turbine = AxialTurbine(duty, efficiency=0.75,
                       stages=[Stage(flow_coefficient=0.6, reaction=0.5)],
                       mean_diameter=0.16,
                       rows=RowSettings(aspect_ratio=1.5, zweifel=0.8, tip_clearance=3e-4))
geometry = turbine.definition.build_geometry()

scene = Scene(title="Turbine")
scene.add(TurbineComponent(geometry))
scene.save_html("turbine.html")

The duty¶

TurbineDuty.from_states takes a medium, a mass flow, the inlet total state, the outlet pressure and the shaft speed. TurbineDuty.from_stations takes the same from two network stations. fluid is a CoolProp name, a thermo.Fluid, or any thermo.EquationOfState.

The duty reports the isentropic head isentropic_head, pressure_ratio, the spouting velocity c0 = sqrt(2*Δh_s), the inlet and isentropic-outlet flows and densities, the specific speed Ns, and blade_jet_ratio(D) and specific_diameter(D) for a candidate diameter.

outlet_basis says whether the outlet pressure is total ('total', the default, and what network stations carry) or static ('static'). The efficiency a design is given is read on the same basis. Total-to-static, the exit kinetic energy counts as loss, so a design refuses an efficiency that leaves less loss than the exit velocity carries away.

The meanline¶

The design runs at one mean radius through the whole turbine, so the blade speed u is the same at every station. Give either mean_diameter or blade_jet_ratio (u/c0).

Each Stage is a stator and a rotor. Its flow_coefficient (c_x/u) sets the axial velocity through it. It also takes one of two things: reaction (the rotor’s share of the stage’s static enthalpy drop) or exit_swirl (the absolute swirl leaving the rotor, in m/s). The other follows from it.

Each stage does its work_split share of efficiency * Δh_s. The efficiency is an input. Where it comes from is up to you, and the design does not check it against anything. It enters the design twice:

  • in the work, and so in how far the rotor turns the flow;

  • through the entropy rise it implies, which the design shares among the rows (evenly, or by loss_split). Each station’s static state follows from its enthalpy and that entropy, and continuity at that density gives the blade height.

turbine.stations holds the flow at every row edge: axial and swirl velocity, absolute and relative angles, static state, total pressure, and absolute and relative Mach numbers. turbine.stages gives each stage’s reaction, flow coefficient, loading and Euler work. turbine.exit_swirl and turbine.exit_flow_angle describe the flow a downstream duct or collector receives.

Angles are radians from axial, positive in the direction the rotor turns.

From triangles to blades¶

RowSettings applies to every row:

Setting

Meaning

aspect_ratio

Mean blade height over true chord.

zweifel or pitch_chord

Blade spacing: from Zweifel’s loading coefficient (default 0.8) or directly.

incidence_deg, deviation_deg

Metal angle minus flow angle at the inlet; how far the leaving flow falls short of the outlet metal.

stator, rotor

A SectionProfile for each kind of row: see below.

axial_gap

Space between rows, over the upstream row’s axial chord.

inlet_length, outlet_length

Straight annulus before the first row and after the last, over that row’s axial chord.

tip_clearance

Rotor tip gap [m]. Zero is a shrouded rotor.

hub_clearance

Gap under a cantilevered stator, over a rotating drum [m].

Blade heights follow continuity at constant mean radius, and every row twists as a free vortex from hub to tip.

A SectionProfile is a section’s shape, in fractions of its chord:

Setting

Meaning

max_thickness, leading_edge_diameter, trailing_edge_thickness

Thicknesses, over chord.

trailing_edge_wedge_deg

Suction- and pressure-side wedge angles at the trailing edge.

distribution

A ThicknessDistribution: suction- and pressure-side control-point offsets along the camber line, how far along it they reach, and how they are spaced. The default is front-loaded, as a reaction blade or nozzle vane is; ThicknessDistribution.symmetric() peaks at mid-chord.

stagger_fraction

Where the chord line sits between the inlet (0) and outlet (1) metal angles. Default 0.5, their mean.

stagger_deg

Stagger at mean radius, instead of stagger_fraction.

SectionProfile.impulse() is a starting point for impulse rotor blades: symmetric thickness and equally thin edges.

The definition¶

turbine.definition is a TurbineDefinition: hardware only. It holds a flat tuple of BladeRow (count, axial position and chord, annulus radii, metal angles and stagger at mean radius, thicknesses, clearances, admission, and a twist law), plus the MeridionalPassage through them. The passage records which walls turn and which blade gaps exist:

  • the hub turns under each rotor;

  • the casing turns over a shrouded rotor;

  • a rotor tip gap is recorded against the casing, and a cantilevered stator’s gap against the drum under it.

A definition can also be written by hand, with no meanline behind it:

from pyskyfire.turbine import BladeRow, TurbineDefinition

definition = TurbineDefinition(rows=(BladeRow(...), BladeRow(...)),
                               rotation="clockwise", outlet_length=0.004)

definition.exit_annulus is the passage’s outlet: radii, area and wall directions, for whatever joins it there.

Built geometry and what is measured on it¶

build_geometry(span_points=9, chord_points=129) draws each row’s sections with pyTurbo at their span radii. It stacks them on their centroids and wraps them onto the annulus. A rotor’s tips stop tip_clearance short of the casing. Each RowGeometry holds one blade’s suction and pressure surfaces, in cylindrical (x, r, theta).

These are measured on the built sections rather than given, at every span:

  • throat: the shortest line from the trailing edge to the neighbouring blade. gauging_angle, acos(throat/pitch), is roughly the angle the flow leaves at. Set beside the outlet metal angle, it shows how far the drawn section turns the flow from what the triangles asked for;

  • narrowest and narrowest_at: the narrowest gap anywhere between neighbours, and where along the axial chord it is. inlet_width is the width the flow enters through, pitch*cos(inlet metal angle). pinch is the narrowest gap over the smaller of the inlet width and the throat: below one, the flow speeds up into the pinch and slows again after it, which costs loss. narrowest_height is the blade height at the pinch, for comparing areas rather than widths;

  • max_thickness: the section’s actual thickness.

Stagger decides most of the difference between the gauging and metal angles. A row that turns the flow far from axial needs its chord leaned well towards the outlet angle. With the mean-angle default, a vane turning 70° leaves its flow more than 10° short. Raise stagger_fraction until the measured gauging angles are where you want them. An impulse rotor takes its flow in as steeply as it lets it out, so its inlet is as narrow as its throat. A front-loaded thickness or a thick leading edge then narrows the passage further, just inside the inlet; SectionProfile.impulse() avoids that.

TurbineComponent shows the passage walls and repeats each row’s blade round the wheel, with rotors and stators in different colours.

Plots¶

pyskyfire.viz has four Plotly figures for a turbine, ready for Report.add_figure:

  • plot_velocity_triangles(turbine): each stage’s rotor inlet and exit triangles.

  • plot_turbine_meridional(geometry): the passage, the blade outlines and the wall stretches that turn with the shaft.

  • plot_turbine_cascade(geometry, span_fraction=0.5): every row’s section at one span, unrolled.

  • plot_turbine_spanwise(geometry): metal and gauging angles, throat and pitch, hub to tip.

examples/turbine/turbine_report.py puts all of them and the 3D scene in one report, for a single-stage impulse oxygen turbine with a shrouded rotor.
