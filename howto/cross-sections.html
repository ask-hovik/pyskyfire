Define chamber and heat-exchanger cross sections¶

CrossSectionRounded and CrossSectionSquared support both chamber sectors and independently dimensioned tubes. The geometry, hydraulic properties, and thermal surface selection use the same section objects.

Dimensions and wall thickness¶

All widths describe the internal fluid passage: the straight-line distance between the two sidewall endpoints at the floor or roof. A rounded width is a lobe chord, not its diameter or arc length. Every angle in these inputs uses radians.

Input

Meaning

floor_width, roof_width

Internal endpoint-to-endpoint widths, metres

height

Rounded bottom-to-top height, or squared floor-to-roof reference spacing

wall_thickness

Rounded uniform wall thickness; squared floor thickness

sidewall_half_thickness

Squared thickness of one complete sidewall; half the full shared rib between adjacent channels

roof_thickness

Squared roof thickness; defaults to the floor thickness

radius, angle

Alternative occupied-sector construction; angle includes the material allotted to the sidewalls

An existing 1 mm full squared rib becomes CrossSectionSquared(sidewall_half_thickness=0.5e-3). The old full-rib keyword has been removed; convert constants and callable return values by dividing by two. The separate internal rounded class has also been removed. Select the desired thermal surfaces on CrossSectionRounded instead.

Local exchanger profiles¶

Use SectionProfiles.from_dimensions without a thrust chamber or placed path. Scalars and one-dimensional arrays broadcast over the supplied stations:

import numpy as np
from pyskyfire.regen import CrossSectionRounded, CrossSectionSquared, SectionProfiles

profiles = SectionProfiles.from_dimensions(
    stations=np.linspace(0, 0.2, 11),
    floor_width=0.006,
    roof_width=0.006,
    height=0.010,
    wall_thickness=0.0005,
)

rounded = CrossSectionRounded(exposure={"floor": 1, "roof": 1})
rectangle = CrossSectionSquared(
    sidewall_half_thickness=0.0004,
    roof_thickness=0.0008,
    floor="line",
    roof="line",
    exposure={"floor": 1, "roof": 1},
)

flow_area = rounded.A_coolant(profiles)
hydraulic_diameter = rounded.Dh_coolant(profiles)
outer_area = rectangle.A_exterior(profiles)
floor_width, roof_width = rounded.widths(profiles)

A_exterior includes both wall material and enclosed fluid. Inner and outer curves are available through boundary, outer_boundary, and the named surfaces mapping. Their coordinates are in the local section plane; placement onto a path is a separate operation.

Equal rounded widths give parallel sidewalls. At full height equal to width, the passage becomes a circle. Unequal widths give different circular lobes joined tangentially. Some width/height combinations are impossible and raise ValueError. If two tangent constructions are possible, full-height input chooses the one with the smaller sidewall angle. height_input="base" instead interprets height as the distance between lobe centres and determines that angle directly.

For squared profiles, choose floor="arc" or "line" independently of roof. With direct widths and finite radius, the floor arc radius is radius and the roof arc radius is radius + height; their midpoints are separated by height. Straight ends use the same widths and midpoint spacing. Omitting radius, or setting it to infinity, produces exact lines. No large artificial radius is needed to approximate a rectangle. A batch of arc profiles must use either all finite or all infinite radii.

Squared exterior corners are intersections of the normally offset floor, roof, and sidewalls. Rounded exterior surfaces all use the same uniform wall offset, retaining tangency.

Occupied sectors and existing chamber construction¶

Alternatively, supply radius, angle, and height, without either width:

sector = SectionProfiles.from_dimensions(
    radius=0.10,
    angle=0.10,
    height=0.012,
    wall_thickness=0.001,
)
section = CrossSectionSquared(sidewall_half_thickness=0.0005)
floor_width, roof_width = section.widths(sector)

The sector construction subtracts the sidewall material before resolving the internal widths. Rounded channels use the uniform wall thickness; squared channels use sidewall_half_thickness. Both routes then construct the same endpoint-based section geometry as direct widths.

As in chamber profiles, sector radius locates the internal floor midpoint for squared sections and the lower lobe centre for rounded sections. It is independent of where an exchanger will eventually be placed in world space.

For sector-based squared channels, switching an end from arc to line joins the same sidewall endpoints with a chord. Thus it preserves the existing sidewall locations and material allocation. The input height remains the spacing between the floor and roof reference radii; full_height(profiles) reports the actual spacing between the selected curves’ midpoints.

CoolingCircuit continues to supply the section profiles from its contour, placement, channel height, and wall stack. Rounded profiles retain the exact sidewall angle specified by this construction, including circular limits. The established projection of helical squared channels is retained; see Physical cooling-channel construction for its material-thickness convention.

Select thermal surfaces¶

The four surface names are floor, roof, side_left, and side_right. Each accepts a centred exposed fraction from 0 to 1:

section = CrossSectionSquared(
    sidewall_half_thickness=0.0005,
    exposure={"floor": 0.75, "roof": 0.5, "side_left": 1.0},
)

Unspecified surfaces are inactive. With no exposure arguments the complete floor is active. An explicit empty mapping disables all direct heat transfer. These choices change thermal perimeters; flow area and the full wetted perimeter used in hydraulic diameter are independent of exposure.

Rounded floor and roof arcs may instead specify a centred angle in radians:

chamber_section = CrossSectionRounded(
    exposure_angles={"floor": 112 * np.pi / 180},
)

This explicitly reproduces the former 112-degree contact model. The angle is now user input, and the examples and RL10 validation supply it themselves. An angle larger than its available lobe arc raises. A surface cannot specify both a fraction and an angle.

thermal_perimeters(profiles) returns each active internal length; exterior=True selects the outer side. P_coolant and P_thermal provide the totals used by the existing regen interface. The established squared chamber floor-area approximation is retained for sector-based squared floor arcs; the other surfaces use their geometric lengths.

Exposure values and squared sidewall/roof thicknesses can use design-input callables. For local profiles, x is the supplied station coordinate; it is not a world or chamber position. For example, exposure={"floor": lambda x: 0.5 if x < 0.1 else 1.0} changes the active fraction along a tube.

Symmetric ribs and fins¶

Set enable_fin=True on an independent section to include the existing symmetric sidewall rib model in R_coolant_per_len. It represents half a shared rib on each side of a channel. The conduction calculation therefore uses twice the individual sidewall thickness as the full rib thickness.

section = CrossSectionSquared(sidewall_half_thickness=0.0005, enable_fin=True)
resistance = section.R_coolant_per_len(profiles, h_c=1500.0, k_wall=300.0)

There is one fin switch for both sides. Any nonzero direct exposure on either sidewall conflicts with fin modeling and raises. A CoolingCircuit supplies its own existing enable_fin setting when calculating resistance, allowing several circuits to share a section definition with different fin settings.

This change supplies section geometry and thermal surface accounting. A general two-fluid exchanger solver can consume these interfaces in a subsequent step.
