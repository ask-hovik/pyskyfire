Instantaneous CTE [1/K], the derivative of a thermal-strain cubic in (T - 273).

Chen et al. fit strain over 20-1000 °C; their printed CTE equations are garbled, so the CTE is taken from the strain fit instead.

pyskyfire.common.solids.ArrayLike¶
pyskyfire.common.solids.GRCop42¶
pyskyfire.common.solids.GRCop42_AM¶

GRCop-42 built by laser powder bed fusion, from Chen et al. (2023).

Conductivity is measured over 298-973 K, held constant below and extended linearly above to the solidus. About 85 % of pure copper at room temperature.

pyskyfire.common.solids.GRCop84_AM¶

GRCop-84 built by laser powder bed fusion, from Chen et al. (2023).

Conductivity is the mean of two builds over 298-973 K, held constant below and extended linearly above to the solidus. Modulus, Poisson’s ratio and density are not given by the source and are left unset.

pyskyfire.common.solids.Inconel625¶

Ni-based superalloy with excellent corrosion resistance and high-temp strength. Typical use: hot-section hardware, manifolds, and fasteners. Density ~8.44 g/cc.

pyskyfire.common.solids.Inconel718¶
pyskyfire.common.solids.L0 = 2.44e-08¶
pyskyfire.common.solids.PureSilver¶
pyskyfire.common.solids.SilicaDeposit¶

Silica laid down on the hot wall by a silicon fuel additive such as TEOS.

Conductivity is that of dense vitreous silica without radiative transfer, from Carwile and Hoge (1966): 1.37 W/m·K at 300 K, 1.79 W/m·K at 660 K and about 2.1 W/m·K at 1500 K. Below 660 K it is measured; above, it is their estimate.

No measured conductivity of an additive-formed deposit was found. Such a deposit is likely porous and then conducts less than this. Only the thermal resistance t/k of the layer enters the solve, so read its thickness as the thickness of dense silica with the same resistance, and calibrate it against a measured drop in heat load. Use it as a role="liner" wall ahead of the chamber wall. Density is left unset, since a porous deposit’s is not known.
